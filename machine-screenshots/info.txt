This directory contains screenshots from various machines running various Infocom retail games (via emulation). This is to capture the official look and feel of these games for comparison purposes.


THE DOS CAPTURES (dos-*.png) ARE WHAT PINNED INTERPRETER 6 (SQ-0928)

`zvm::interpreter`'s IBM PC row stated no default page or ink for months, on the
reasoning that "an IBM PC in a terminal is the player's terminal". These three are
why it now states blue 6 under white 9 — and why interpreter 6 was the last gap
SQ-0873 left open, alongside the Atari ST.

  dos-shogun.png     Shogun r322 / Pix 21 / 890706, "IBM Interpreter version 6.68",
                     at the main menu.  ~44% #0000A1..A6 — EGA blue (#0000AA,
                     colour 1) with video-scaling jitter — under white text.
  dos-arthur.png     Arthur r74, mid-game (Churchyard, St Anne's Day, Compline).
                     40% #0000A1. Same blue.
  dos-zorkzero.png   Zork Zero at the Banquet Hall. 25.7% #FFFFFF and near-whites:
                     WHITE, not blue.

The three do not contradict each other, and working out why is the finding. A
trace of every `@set_colour` each game issues says: Arthur (941 screen ops) and
Journey name no colour at all; Shogun names one, on a 548x32 status strip; Zork
Zero names one on a window the size of the screen. So blue is the interpreter's
own page, games layer per-window pairs on top of it, and only Zork Zero colours a
window big enough to replace the screen. The user also reports that in the source
video Zork Zero's BOOT sequence is blue/white before the game runs — i.e. before
it can have set anything, which is what turns a coincidence into a default.

A DISCARDED CAPTURE, recorded so nobody rediscovers it: an earlier Arthur frame
from a different walkthrough showed a GREY ground and appeared to refute all of
this. Arthur names no colour and its art canvas is only 40% opaque (the knotwork
border), so the grey was neither declared nor drawn. That video was not true DOS
emulation. **A walkthrough recording is not a machine capture** — it may be a
modern interpreter reporting itself as an IBM.

What is pinned from these is a section 8.3.1 colour NUMBER, not a shade, so unlike
the period-look values (SQ-0873) there is no emulator-dependent RGB here to be
wrong about.


dos-hitchhiker.png IS THE ONE THAT ANSWERS SQ-0873 FOR INTERPRETER 6

Hitchhiker's r47 / 840914 — **Version 3**, on DOS with a CGA colour display. That
version matters more than the machine: colour arrives with v5, so a v3 story has
no colour concept at all and every pixel here is the interpreter's own
presentation. This is a PERIOD LOOK capture, which is exactly what SQ-0873 lacked
for interpreter 6 and why that row declines one.

Measured, row- and column-censused rather than sampled:

  body        page #0F009E (92.4% of the frame), ink #A0A0A0 — but see below:
              what the TABLE records is the hardware's #0000AA / #AAAAAA
  status row  NOT a band: 611 contiguous px of the blue PAGE run through the
              middle of it (x 132..743 at y=14). The location sits on a grey
              ground; the labels are ink on the page.
  and         #A2000D — RED — appears in the status row and NOWHERE else in the
              frame (0.1% overall). It is in neither channel of the body pair.

TWO THINGS FOLLOW, and the second is a problem for the type as it stands.

1. It corroborates SQ-0928 from a different direction. That quest's evidence is
   v6 (Shogun, Arthur, Zork Zero); this is v3, a decade of interpreter apart, and
   the page is the same blue. What v6 games are TOLD in $2C/$2D and what a v3
   game is SHOWN agree, which is what you would expect of a real default and not
   of a coincidence.

2. `zvm::interpreter::StatusBand` cannot express this. Its four variants are
   FullReverse, PerRun, Own{ground,ink} and Ruled. This frame is PerRun in
   structure — the page shows between the runs — and Own in colour, since red is
   not the body pair reversed. Adding interpreter 6's period look therefore needs
   a variant that carries both, or the four have to become two axes. Do not force
   it into PerRun and lose the red.

CAVEAT worth keeping: the capture is 1159x726, a non-integer scale of a 640x200
or 720x350 text screen, so glyph edges interpolate and the run boundaries above
are ±2px. The three COLOURS are unambiguous; the exact cell boundaries are not.

A NOTE ON THE RGB IN THESE DOS CAPTURES, because the table does not use it

`zvm::interpreter`'s IBM PC period look records **#0000AA / #AAAAAA** — EGA/CGA
palette entries 1 and 7 out of `blorb::infocom_pics::EGA_PALETTE`, which is
verified there against four independent sources — and NOT the #0F009E / #A0A0A0
this capture measures.

That is the opposite of what every other period-look row does, and the reason is
that this is the one machine whose palette is DIGITAL and exactly specified. The
VIC-II's is analogue, which is why Pepto, Colodore and VICE's default disagree on
the Commodore 64's grey; the Amiga's #074BA1 is an emulator's rendering of what is
almost certainly register $05A (bit-replicating to #0055AA). Those rows have to
record what was drawn because nothing better exists. This one does not.

The gap is video scaling and it is visible across the corpus: dos-shogun.png and
dos-arthur.png measure #0000A1..A6 with no red tint, while dos-hitchhiker.png's
#0F009E carries 0x0F of red that no EGA colour has. Three captures of one palette
entry, three answers, none of them the palette.


st-zork1.png CLOSES THE TABLE — INTERPRETER 5, THE LAST UNMEASURED MACHINE

Zork I revision 88 / serial 840726 on an Atari ST, at the opening turn (West of
House, Score 0/1) — the same release stories/ carries. Version 3, so the story
has no colour concept whatsoever and every pixel in the frame is the
interpreter's own presentation. That is what makes it a PERIOD LOOK capture in
exactly the sense dos-hitchhiker.png is, and it is the frame that emptied
zvm::interpreter's `the_machines_with_no_capture_decline`.

READ THE SCANLINE FILTER FIRST. IT DOES NOT DIM THE ANSWER, IT INVERTS IT.

The emulator drew every other line black. Censused whole, the frame is 53.6%
#000000 against 45.9% #EBEBEB — which says the page is BLACK. Split by row
parity it says the opposite, and unambiguously:

  even rows   99.2% pure #000000          <- the gaps between scanlines
  odd rows    92.0% #EBEBEB, 7.7% black   <- the picture

So the page is white and the ink is black, and a census that does not ask about
row parity gets the machine exactly backwards. Anything measured off this file
must be measured on the odd rows alone.

WHAT THE CONTENT ROWS SAY

  page/ink    white page, black ink (see the RGB note below)
  status      a FULL-WIDTH REVERSE band, 8 rows tall: x=0 and x=639 both inked
              on every row of it, the last row solid across, with "West of
              House" and "Score: 0/1" reversed out of it in the page colour.
  cursor      a solid filled cell, 8x8, immediately right of the `>` prompt —
              a BLOCK, drawn in the ink.

Corroborated from the other direction, which is why the row is stated rather
than hedged: the ST's v5 pair has been in the table since SQ-0835, read out of
Infocom's own `st/` interpreter as DEF_BACK 9 under DEF_FORE 2 — white and
black. What a v5 story is TOLD and what a v3 story is SHOWN agree, the same
two-channel agreement that settled the IBM PC.

A GEOMETRY FINDING THAT IS NOT IN THE TABLE, RECORDED SO IT IS NOT REDISCOVERED

The capture is 642x401 and looks like a 640x400 screen. It is not. There are 200
content rows, and glyphs dumped off consecutive content rows come out whole —
complete, coherent 8x8 letterforms, not a 16-row face sampled every other line.
The status band is 8 content rows. So this is the ST's MEDIUM resolution,
640x200 on an 8x8 cell, 80x25 characters, displayed at 400 lines by the
emulator's line doubling. Had the glyphs come back broken it would have been
640x400 high-res mono with half the picture missing, and every row measurement
above would have been off by a factor of two.

None of that reaches `MachineProfile`: Zork I is v3 and has no pixel geometry to
read, and `v6_font_cell` deliberately declines to model a per-machine cell (see
its docs, and SQ-0917). It is here because it is the sort of thing the next
person measures wrong.

A NOTE ON THE RGB, BECAUSE THE TABLE AGAIN DOES NOT USE THE CAPTURE'S

zvm::interpreter's Atari ST period look records #FFFFFF / #000000, not the
#EBEBEB the content rows measure. Same judgement as the IBM PC row's #0000AA
over dos-hitchhiker.png's #0F009E: the dimming belongs to the emulator's
scanline filter, and standard colours 9 and 2 are white and black with no shade
to be wrong about. The rows that DO record what was drawn — the Amiga's
#074BA1, the C64's greys — do so because those machines' palettes are analogue
or in doubt and nothing better exists. This one has nothing to be uncertain
about.


dos-zorkzero-cga.png IS THE ONE THAT SAYS A TWO-COLOUR RENDITION HAS ITS OWN PAIR

Zork Zero r393 at the Banquet Hall with [MORE] showing, in a DOS emulator in CGA
mode, running the CGA plates (`zork0.cg1`) rather than the EGA/MCGA ones. It was
captured to answer a question the corpus could not: every other DOS capture here
is a colour rendition, so nothing in the directory could say what the two-colour
one presented on.

Censused whole, 507x317:

  page   48.3% #000000
  ink     8.8% #A0A0A0, and 161 distinct colours — a grey ramp from video
          scaling, with no second hue anywhere in the frame

Row parity was checked FIRST, because st-zork1.png taught this directory that an
interlaced capture censuses backwards: even rows 39,252 black / 7,135 grey, odd
rows 38,391 / 6,968. They agree, so this one is not interlaced and the
whole-frame census is the honest reading.

WHAT IT SETTLES, AND IT IS NOT MERELY "THE BACKGROUND WAS BLACK"

Zork Zero issues `set_colour(fg=2, bg=9)` — black ink on a white page — for every
video card alike, and dos-zorkzero.png shows that honoured on the colour
rendition (25.7% #FFFFFF). This frame is the OPPOSITE POLARITY: light ink on a
black page. So a two-colour display does not substitute a different background;
the pair reaches it as ONE BIT — which of the two channels wants the lit state —
and the card shows its own two colours in that polarity. That is
`zvm::screen::two_colour_card_request`, and SQ-0956's notes are where the
reasoning sits. Turning the story's colours off (SQ-0806) reproduces this same
frame, because the game checks the colour flag before it does any colour work at
all — but it also disables Zork Zero's in-game `color` command, which on this
machine offers exactly the swap the bit describes.

In colour NUMBERS rather than shades, which is what this directory pins: page 0
(black) under ink 7 (light grey). #A0A0A0 is the same value dos-hitchhiker.png
measures for ITS ink, and the IBM PC row already records #AAAAAA as EGA/CGA
entry 7 — two captures from the same emulator family agreeing is what makes this
a number and not an artifact. Same judgement as the RGB notes above.

AND IT PAIRS WITH THE MACINTOSH TO SHOW THE POLARITY IS PER MACHINE

  Macintosh mono Pic.data  ->  WHITE page, BLACK ink  (mac/xzip.lst's
                               SetColor := (zWHITE*256) + zBLACK)
  IBM PC .CG1              ->  BLACK page, LIGHT ink  (this file)

Two two-colour archives, opposite polarity — and
`blorb::infocom_pics::InfocomPics::is_monochrome` answers **true** for both, by
its own documentation ("the Macintosh's monochrome Pic.data answers yes under
exactly the same test as a .cg1"). That boolean is where the two machines are
flattened together; the CONTAINER is what tells them apart, and since SQ-0956 they
resolve through two tables — CGA_PALETTE and MAC_MONO_PALETTE — rather than one.

RECONCILED, SQ-0956: CGA_PALETTE's lit state was pure white while this capture's
ink is colour 7, and sampling the frame by region settles it — status text
(160,160,160) over 1850 px, left pillar art (160,160,160) over 1570 px. The SAME
shade for the ink and the artwork, because the card has two colours and everything
on the screen is one of them. The table now says #AAAAAA, and the Macintosh's mono
plate — whose white really is white, see mac-zorkzero-game.png, 77.2% #FFFFFF and
22.8% #000000 inside the 480x300 game window and NOTHING else — moved to a table of its own rather than sharing this one.


THE FOUR SQ-0917 FONT-METRIC CAPTURES (2026-08-22)

  amiga-arthur.png       Arthur on an Amiga, gameplay ("Town square, St John's
                         Day, Terce"). 320x200.
  amiga-arthur-hint.png  The same game's InvisiClues screen. 720x569.
  mac-zorkzero-game.png  Zork Zero on a compact Macintosh, BLACK AND WHITE press,
                         at the Banquet Hall. 552x385.
  mac-zorkzero-hint.png  The same press's InvisiClues topic list. 552x382.

These exist to answer one question the repo could not answer internally: what
CELL did each machine lay Version 6 text out on? babelmap advertises 8x16 at
header $26/$27 and quantizes every v6 coordinate by it. `v6_font_cell` declined to
change that three times (EGA SQ-0790, Macintosh SQ-0838, Apple IIgs SQ-0857) on
the reasoning that nothing depended on it, and SQ-0917 is where something did.

TWO OF THEM ARE PIXEL-EXACT. TWO ARE NOT. THE DIFFERENCE IS THE WHOLE POINT.

  amiga-arthur.png       18 colours, every channel of every one a multiple of
                         0x11 (0x44, 0x55, 0x99, 0xDD...). That is a 4-bit-per-
                         channel OCS palette with no blended value anywhere. A
                         resampled or video-captured frame carries off-palette
                         averages; this one carries none, so it is native pixels.
  mac-zorkzero-*.png     1-bit. The hint capture has literally TWO colours. The
                         uniform outer border resolves to a content rect of
                         exactly 512x342 — a compact Macintosh screen (Plus / SE
                         / Classic) inside a 20 px emulator frame. 1:1.
  amiga-arthur-hint.png  720x569 with off-palette greys, and its text pitch is
                         20 px against the gameplay capture's 10. A DOUBLED
                         rendition of the same 320x200 screen, not a native hires
                         one. Good for layout, z-order and the SQ-1004 island
                         defect; normalise before quoting a metric from it.

DO NOT MEASURE METRICS FROM THE REMAINING mac-*.jpg FILES. The now-deleted
`mac-arthur.jpg` was the lesson: lossy, windowed, and its advance-to-line-height
ratio came out 5.6/14 = 0.40 where the Mac's own colWidth/lineHeight is 7/15 =
0.47 and babelmap's is 8/16 = 0.50. Something was scaled by a factor the file
could not tell you about. The PNG that replaced it reads 7 and 15 cleanly, and
its insertion caret is 15 rows where the JPEG's read 13 — the same scaling, seen
from another angle. The three JPEGs still here are good for colour and for gross
layout, and for nothing that has a number in it.

THE MACINTOSH: 480x300 ON A 15-ROW LINE, DRAWN PROPORTIONALLY

The window frame inside the 512x342 desktop sits at desktop-relative rows 19
(top), 23-37 (title bar) and 338-339 (bottom), columns 11 and 500-501. The
content is exactly 300 ROWS tall and ~480 wide after the inner inset — which is
`Pic.data`'s 480x300 (SQ-0838), confirmed from outside for the first time.

The line height is 15, three independent ways:

  1. The inverse highlight bar on PROLOGUE spans rows 118..132 — exactly 15 rows.
     An inverse bar is drawn to the CELL, so this measures the cell and not ink.
  2. All fifteen topic rows land on y = 118 + 15*i, a formula that indexed 14
     consecutive lines correctly.
  3. Banner baselines are 15 apart (84 -> 99). And the GAMEPLAY capture's prose
     has the same 15 px pitch (line tops 241, 256, 271, 285), so it is not a
     hint-screen peculiarity.

Infocom's Mac interpreter reads `lineHeight := 15 {16}`. It is 15.

AND THE FACE IS PROPORTIONAL, WHICH IS THE FINDING

The test controls for glyph identity: WING is the same four-glyph run in three
different topics.

  EAST WING      W at char index 5   x = 137   WING inks 30 px
  WEST WING      W at char index 5   x = 139   WING inks 30 px
  SECRET WING    W at char index 7   x = 151   WING inks 30 px

Same run, same index 5, DIFFERENT x — 137 against 139 — because WEST is 2 px
wider than EAST. Under any fixed pitch those two must be equal. The measurement
is sound in both directions: identical strings do have identical widths (WING
inks to 30 px in all three rows, AREA to 31 px in all three of its rows).

But the AVERAGE advance is 7.00. WING at index 5 (x=137) against index 7 (x=151)
is 14 px over two characters. BAILEY/VILLAGE AREA puts AREA at index 15, x=207 —
106 px over 15 characters, 7.07.

That resolves what looks like a contradiction with `native_disk_font.rs`, which
reads FONT 524 as fixed-pitch. Infocom's Mac source is `stdFont := geneva;
textSize (12); lineHeight := 15 {16}; colWidth := 7`, and Geneva 12 is a
PROPORTIONAL System font. The interpreter draws proportional Geneva and DECLARES
a nominal column width of 7 — 7 being Geneva's average, which is why the two
agree. `colWidth := 7` was never a drawn advance; it is a declared metric, the
same quantity as header $27.

So the Macintosh needs the CELL and not the glyphs. Wiring FONT 524 into the
renderer would be wrong on its own terms, because the real interpreter did not
tell the story about proportional widths either. It told it 7.

A SECOND, SMALLER FACE ON THE BANNER — INTERPRETER CHROME, NOT A Z-MACHINE FONT

The InvisiClues banner ("Hit N for next item." / "Hit Return to see hints.") is
drawn ~6.3 px average against the topic list's 7.0. Same-string widths are
consistent there too — "Hit" inks to 17 px in all four of its occurrences, "to"
to 11 px in both — so it is a real second face and not noise. Presumably Geneva 9
/ FONT 1033 (7x12, advance 6).

The Z-machine cannot express this: fonts 1-4 are normal, picture, character
graphics and fixed-pitch, and there is no smaller face among them. So it is the
Mac interpreter's own chrome and babelmap should not reproduce it — but it does
temper SQ-0917's acceptance test. Going 8 -> 7 removes most of the reported
banner overrun and may not remove all of it. A residual at 7 is this second face,
not a failed cell change.

THE AMIGA: 320x200 ON A 10-ROW LINE, AND GENUINELY PROPORTIONAL

White text pixel rows in amiga-arthur.png run 121-129, 131-139, 141-149, 161-169,
171-179 — pitch exactly 10 — and the inverse status bar occupies rows 100-109,
exactly one cell, independently. Glyphs use about 7 rows with descenders reaching
the 9th.

Prose line 1 is "You walk into the town square. The churchyard lies to the
north," — 64 characters spanning x=14..301, 288 px, 4.5 px/char. Fixed pitch at
the nominal 10 would need 640 px on a 320 px screen, so proportional is not a
detail here; it is the only way the line fits. Segmenting by inter-word gaps:

  You 15px/3=5.00   walk 20/4=5.00   into 16/4=4.00   the 13/3=4.33
  town 21/4=5.25    square. 32/7=4.57   The 15/3=5.00   churchyard 50/10=5.00
  lies 15/4=3.75    to 8/2=4.00   the 13/3=4.33   north, 26/6=4.33

A fixed-pitch face gives every word the SAME px/char. These range 3.75 to 5.25
and track word content (lies, all narrow letters, lowest; town, with its w,
highest). That is a falsification and not merely a consistency check.

So `native_disk_font.rs`'s `arthur_carries_a_proportional_text_font` — 10x10
nominal, FPF_PROPORTIONAL set, advance 2..=8, read from char.data on the r54 /
890606 floppy — now agrees with a real machine and not only with its own parser.

Band structure of the gameplay frame, for anyone pinning geometry against it:
rows 0-6 grey ground; 7-90 knotwork border and illustration; 91-99 grey; 100-109
the inverse status bar (one cell); 110-120 grey; 121-179 prose at 10 px pitch;
190-199 the input line. A green vertical rule runs down x~314-319 full height.

A SEPARATE FINDING: AMIGA TEXT IS 25% LARGER RELATIVE TO ITS ART THAN OURS

AMIGA_STD_WINDOW is (320, 200) and V6_ART_SCALE doubles it onto the 640x400 unit
screen, where the 8x16 cell gives 80x25 characters. The machine in this capture
has 20 text rows, not 25. As a ratio, which does not depend on the doubling:
babelmap's line is 16/400 = 4.0% of screen height, the Amiga's is 10/200 = 5.0%.

This is NOT a defect claim and should not be actioned from this note alone. The
2x unit-screen model is deliberate (SQ-0479) and Arthur's windows are laid out
from what the story is told, so a taller grid is not automatically a wrong one.
It is recorded because it is now measurable against a machine, and because it
says the Macintosh is the outlier by a wide margin while the Amiga is merely a
bit small.


AND THE COLOUR MACINTOSH PRESS, WHICH CLOSES THE PAIR (2026-08-22)

  mac-zorkzero.png   Zork Zero, COLOUR press, at the Banquet Hall. 652x424.
  mac-arthur.png     Arthur, COLOUR press. 652x423.

Both are window captures rather than whole desktops. `mac-journey.png` joined
them the same day. Together with the mono pair they SUPERSEDED four JPEGs of the
same game and press, which were deleted rather than left as traps — a lossy file
that cannot be measured from, sitting beside a PNG that can, gets measured from.

Three JPEGs remain, because nothing supersedes them: `mac-shogun.jpg` (colour
press), `mac-shogun-bw.jpg` (mono press) and `mac-zork1.jpg` (Version 3). They
are still only good for colour and gross layout. If a PNG of any of them ever
arrives, delete the JPEG in the same commit.

The question these answer is whether 7x15 is the b/w press's cell or the
MACHINE's. It is the machine's, and `mac/xzip.lst` says so in one statement:

    totRows := (bottom - top) {DIV lineheight};
    totCols := ((right - left) - (2 * wMarg)) {DIV colWidth};

One code path, one lineHeight, one colWidth. What the `myBig` branch varies is
the window RECT it divides, not the cell it divides by:

  CPic.data (colour)   320x200 art at 2x   ->  640x400 screen   ->  91 x 26 chars
  Pic.data  (b&w)      480x300 art at 1x   ->  480x300 screen   ->  68 x 20 chars

Measured, and it holds:

  mac-arthur.png     content y 20..419 = 400 ROWS exactly, x 2..649 = 648 wide
  mac-zorkzero-*.png content y 38..337 = 300 ROWS exactly, x 12..499 = 488 wide

Both are 8 px wider than the interpreter's `wx`, which is `2 * wMarg` with
wMarg = 4 — exactly the term totCols subtracts before dividing. 648-8 = 640 and
488-8 = 480, the two `wx` values above.

The colour prose confirms the line height independently: ascender tops at 266,
281, 296, 311, 326 and 341, five consecutive gaps of 15. The b/w capture's
PROLOGUE inverse bar says 15 from the other direction. Same cell, both presses.

NOTE THE DIRECTION OF THAT PASCAL, IT IS AN IMPLEMENTATION CONSTRAINT

totCols is derived FROM the window, and subtracts a margin before dividing.
babelmap goes the other way — `session.rs` computes cols from pixels and
`write_screen_dims` re-multiplies cols by the cell back into $22. That round trip
is lossless at 8 (480/8 = 60, 60*8 = 480) and LOSSY at 7 (480/7 = 68, 68*7 =
476). So the v6 path has to carry the pixel screen as primary and derive the
grid from it, the way the Mac does. SQ-0917 is not a constant swap.
