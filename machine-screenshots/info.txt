This directory contains screenshots from various machines running various Infocom retail games (via emulation). This is to capture the official look and feel of these games for comparison purposes.


THE DOS CAPTURES (dos-*.png) ARE WHAT PINNED INTERPRETER 6 (SQ-0928)

`zvm::interpreter`'s IBM PC row stated no default page or ink for months, on the
reasoning that "an IBM PC in a terminal is the player's terminal". These three are
why it now states blue 6 under white 9 — and why interpreter 6 was the last gap
SQ-0873 left open, alongside the Atari ST.

  dos-shogun.png     Shogun r322 / Pix 21 / 890706, "IBM Interpreter version 6.68",
                     at the main menu.  ~44% #0000A1..A6 — EGA blue (#0000AA,
                     colour 1) with video-scaling jitter — under white text.
  dos-arthur.png     Arthur r74, mid-game (Churchyard, St Anne's Day, Compline).
                     40% #0000A1. Same blue.
  dos-zorkzero.png   Zork Zero at the Banquet Hall. 25.7% #FFFFFF and near-whites:
                     WHITE, not blue.

The three do not contradict each other, and working out why is the finding. A
trace of every `@set_colour` each game issues says: Arthur (941 screen ops) and
Journey name no colour at all; Shogun names one, on a 548x32 status strip; Zork
Zero names one on a window the size of the screen. So blue is the interpreter's
own page, games layer per-window pairs on top of it, and only Zork Zero colours a
window big enough to replace the screen. The user also reports that in the source
video Zork Zero's BOOT sequence is blue/white before the game runs — i.e. before
it can have set anything, which is what turns a coincidence into a default.

A DISCARDED CAPTURE, recorded so nobody rediscovers it: an earlier Arthur frame
from a different walkthrough showed a GREY ground and appeared to refute all of
this. Arthur names no colour and its art canvas is only 40% opaque (the knotwork
border), so the grey was neither declared nor drawn. That video was not true DOS
emulation. **A walkthrough recording is not a machine capture** — it may be a
modern interpreter reporting itself as an IBM.

What is pinned from these is a section 8.3.1 colour NUMBER, not a shade, so unlike
the period-look values (SQ-0873) there is no emulator-dependent RGB here to be
wrong about.


dos-hitchhiker.png IS THE ONE THAT ANSWERS SQ-0873 FOR INTERPRETER 6

Hitchhiker's r47 / 840914 — **Version 3**, on DOS with a CGA colour display. That
version matters more than the machine: colour arrives with v5, so a v3 story has
no colour concept at all and every pixel here is the interpreter's own
presentation. This is a PERIOD LOOK capture, which is exactly what SQ-0873 lacked
for interpreter 6 and why that row declines one.

Measured, row- and column-censused rather than sampled:

  body        page #0F009E (92.4% of the frame), ink #A0A0A0 — but see below:
              what the TABLE records is the hardware's #0000AA / #AAAAAA
  status row  NOT a band: 611 contiguous px of the blue PAGE run through the
              middle of it (x 132..743 at y=14). The location sits on a grey
              ground; the labels are ink on the page.
  and         #A2000D — RED — appears in the status row and NOWHERE else in the
              frame (0.1% overall). It is in neither channel of the body pair.

TWO THINGS FOLLOW, and the second is a problem for the type as it stands.

1. It corroborates SQ-0928 from a different direction. That quest's evidence is
   v6 (Shogun, Arthur, Zork Zero); this is v3, a decade of interpreter apart, and
   the page is the same blue. What v6 games are TOLD in $2C/$2D and what a v3
   game is SHOWN agree, which is what you would expect of a real default and not
   of a coincidence.

2. `zvm::interpreter::StatusBand` cannot express this. Its four variants are
   FullReverse, PerRun, Own{ground,ink} and Ruled. This frame is PerRun in
   structure — the page shows between the runs — and Own in colour, since red is
   not the body pair reversed. Adding interpreter 6's period look therefore needs
   a variant that carries both, or the four have to become two axes. Do not force
   it into PerRun and lose the red.

CAVEAT worth keeping: the capture is 1159x726, a non-integer scale of a 640x200
or 720x350 text screen, so glyph edges interpolate and the run boundaries above
are ±2px. The three COLOURS are unambiguous; the exact cell boundaries are not.

A NOTE ON THE RGB IN THESE DOS CAPTURES, because the table does not use it

`zvm::interpreter`'s IBM PC period look records **#0000AA / #AAAAAA** — EGA/CGA
palette entries 1 and 7 out of `blorb::infocom_pics::EGA_PALETTE`, which is
verified there against four independent sources — and NOT the #0F009E / #A0A0A0
this capture measures.

That is the opposite of what every other period-look row does, and the reason is
that this is the one machine whose palette is DIGITAL and exactly specified. The
VIC-II's is analogue, which is why Pepto, Colodore and VICE's default disagree on
the Commodore 64's grey; the Amiga's #074BA1 is an emulator's rendering of what is
almost certainly register $05A (bit-replicating to #0055AA). Those rows have to
record what was drawn because nothing better exists. This one does not.

The gap is video scaling and it is visible across the corpus: dos-shogun.png and
dos-arthur.png measure #0000A1..A6 with no red tint, while dos-hitchhiker.png's
#0F009E carries 0x0F of red that no EGA colour has. Three captures of one palette
entry, three answers, none of them the palette.


st-zork1.png CLOSES THE TABLE — INTERPRETER 5, THE LAST UNMEASURED MACHINE

Zork I revision 88 / serial 840726 on an Atari ST, at the opening turn (West of
House, Score 0/1) — the same release stories/ carries. Version 3, so the story
has no colour concept whatsoever and every pixel in the frame is the
interpreter's own presentation. That is what makes it a PERIOD LOOK capture in
exactly the sense dos-hitchhiker.png is, and it is the frame that emptied
zvm::interpreter's `the_machines_with_no_capture_decline`.

READ THE SCANLINE FILTER FIRST. IT DOES NOT DIM THE ANSWER, IT INVERTS IT.

The emulator drew every other line black. Censused whole, the frame is 53.6%
#000000 against 45.9% #EBEBEB — which says the page is BLACK. Split by row
parity it says the opposite, and unambiguously:

  even rows   99.2% pure #000000          <- the gaps between scanlines
  odd rows    92.0% #EBEBEB, 7.7% black   <- the picture

So the page is white and the ink is black, and a census that does not ask about
row parity gets the machine exactly backwards. Anything measured off this file
must be measured on the odd rows alone.

WHAT THE CONTENT ROWS SAY

  page/ink    white page, black ink (see the RGB note below)
  status      a FULL-WIDTH REVERSE band, 8 rows tall: x=0 and x=639 both inked
              on every row of it, the last row solid across, with "West of
              House" and "Score: 0/1" reversed out of it in the page colour.
  cursor      a solid filled cell, 8x8, immediately right of the `>` prompt —
              a BLOCK, drawn in the ink.

Corroborated from the other direction, which is why the row is stated rather
than hedged: the ST's v5 pair has been in the table since SQ-0835, read out of
Infocom's own `st/` interpreter as DEF_BACK 9 under DEF_FORE 2 — white and
black. What a v5 story is TOLD and what a v3 story is SHOWN agree, the same
two-channel agreement that settled the IBM PC.

A GEOMETRY FINDING THAT IS NOT IN THE TABLE, RECORDED SO IT IS NOT REDISCOVERED

The capture is 642x401 and looks like a 640x400 screen. It is not. There are 200
content rows, and glyphs dumped off consecutive content rows come out whole —
complete, coherent 8x8 letterforms, not a 16-row face sampled every other line.
The status band is 8 content rows. So this is the ST's MEDIUM resolution,
640x200 on an 8x8 cell, 80x25 characters, displayed at 400 lines by the
emulator's line doubling. Had the glyphs come back broken it would have been
640x400 high-res mono with half the picture missing, and every row measurement
above would have been off by a factor of two.

None of that reaches `MachineProfile`: Zork I is v3 and has no pixel geometry to
read, and `v6_font_cell` deliberately declines to model a per-machine cell (see
its docs, and SQ-0917). It is here because it is the sort of thing the next
person measures wrong.

A NOTE ON THE RGB, BECAUSE THE TABLE AGAIN DOES NOT USE THE CAPTURE'S

zvm::interpreter's Atari ST period look records #FFFFFF / #000000, not the
#EBEBEB the content rows measure. Same judgement as the IBM PC row's #0000AA
over dos-hitchhiker.png's #0F009E: the dimming belongs to the emulator's
scanline filter, and standard colours 9 and 2 are white and black with no shade
to be wrong about. The rows that DO record what was drawn — the Amiga's
#074BA1, the C64's greys — do so because those machines' palettes are analogue
or in doubt and nothing better exists. This one has nothing to be uncertain
about.


dos-zorkzero-cga.png IS THE ONE THAT SAYS A TWO-COLOUR RENDITION HAS ITS OWN PAIR

Zork Zero r393 at the Banquet Hall with [MORE] showing, in a DOS emulator in CGA
mode, running the CGA plates (`zork0.cg1`) rather than the EGA/MCGA ones. It was
captured to answer a question the corpus could not: every other DOS capture here
is a colour rendition, so nothing in the directory could say what the two-colour
one presented on.

Censused whole, 507x317:

  page   48.3% #000000
  ink     8.8% #A0A0A0, and 161 distinct colours — a grey ramp from video
          scaling, with no second hue anywhere in the frame

Row parity was checked FIRST, because st-zork1.png taught this directory that an
interlaced capture censuses backwards: even rows 39,252 black / 7,135 grey, odd
rows 38,391 / 6,968. They agree, so this one is not interlaced and the
whole-frame census is the honest reading.

WHAT IT SETTLES, AND IT IS NOT MERELY "THE BACKGROUND WAS BLACK"

Zork Zero issues `set_colour(fg=2, bg=9)` — black ink on a white page — for every
video card alike, and dos-zorkzero.png shows that honoured on the colour
rendition (25.7% #FFFFFF). This frame is the OPPOSITE POLARITY: light ink on a
black page. So a two-colour display does not substitute a different background,
it does not honour the story's pair at all — which is what
`app::graphics::PictSource::declines_game_colours` already asserts in prose and
what SQ-0806 exists for.

In colour NUMBERS rather than shades, which is what this directory pins: page 0
(black) under ink 7 (light grey). #A0A0A0 is the same value dos-hitchhiker.png
measures for ITS ink, and the IBM PC row already records #AAAAAA as EGA/CGA
entry 7 — two captures from the same emulator family agreeing is what makes this
a number and not an artifact. Same judgement as the RGB notes above.

AND IT PAIRS WITH THE MACINTOSH TO SHOW THE POLARITY IS PER MACHINE

  Macintosh mono Pic.data  ->  WHITE page, BLACK ink  (mac/xzip.lst's
                               SetColor := (zWHITE*256) + zBLACK)
  IBM PC .CG1              ->  BLACK page, LIGHT ink  (this file)

Two two-colour archives, opposite polarity — and
`blorb::infocom_pics::InfocomPics::is_monochrome` answers **true** for both, by
its own documentation ("the Macintosh's monochrome Pic.data answers yes under
exactly the same test as a .cg1"). That boolean is where the two machines are
flattened together; `hardware_palette()` is the function that can still tell them
apart. SQ-0956.

One thing left deliberately unreconciled: CGA_PALETTE slot 1 — the packed SET
bit — is pure white, while this capture's ink is colour 7. Mode 6's foreground is
selectable, so both are defensible, and the two should be squared up on purpose
rather than by accident.
